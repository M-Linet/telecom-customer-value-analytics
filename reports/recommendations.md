# Management recommendations

Each recommendation follows from a specific result (query or notebook named). All figures describe the fictional operator Jamii Mobile. Value estimates are run-rate revenue, not margin, and assume the stated recovery share.

| # | Action | Evidence | Estimated value | Owner | Success measure (dashboard KPI) | First step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Contact the 173 declining Platinum and Gold customers this month**: account manager for postpaid, outbound retention call with a tailored bundle for prepaid | q15, q23, notebook 08: 702 declining customers, KES 3.5M a year already lost at run rate, 47% from Platinum/Gold | about KES 0.85M a year if half of the Platinum/Gold decline is recovered | Head of Customer Retention | *Declining Customers* (Platinum/Gold) down by half within one quarter; *Revenue at Risk* falling | Assign the top 50 of `q23` to account managers by county |
| 2 | **7-day target for network and data-speed complaints**, with an escalation path to network engineering in Garissa, Kilifi and Turkana | q17, q19, notebook 07: coverage tickets 12.8 days on average, 29.8% never resolved; complaint rate 27–29 per 1,000 subscriber-months in the three counties vs about 16 elsewhere | reduces the share of customers in the highest-churn complaint group (unresolved: 19–28% churn) | Customer Care Manager + Regional Network Manager | *Avg Days to Resolve* (Network coverage) below 7; *Unresolved Complaint %* below 15% | Weekly review of open network tickets older than 7 days in the three counties |
| 3 | **Treat an unresolved complaint as a churn signal**: any Platinum/Gold complaint open more than 7 days triggers a retention callback | q18: within each value tier, unresolved/escalated complainers churn 1.2–1.5× non-complainers, while resolved complainers churn less | protects the highest-value customers at the moment of greatest risk | Customer Care Manager | callback within 48 hours for 90% of flagged cases; churn of unresolved complainers moving toward the no-complaint rate | Add a daily "open > 7 days, high value" filter to the care queue |
| 4 | **Early-life programme in months 1–3**: welcome bundle, a usage check at day 45, and a reminder before the line goes silent for 90 days | q10, q12, notebook 05: closures peak in tenure months 4–6 (about 3.5% a month); 10% of each cohort is inactive by month 3 | each percentage point of 3-month retention is about 50 customers a year (430 acquisitions a month) | Head of Acquisition / CRM | *Retention %* at k = 3 above 92% for new cohorts | Trigger an SMS offer for customers with no activity 30 days after activation |
| 5 | **Protect low-value prepaid customers in future price changes**: keep an entry-level bundle at the old price point and pilot changes in one region first | q25, notebook 08: prepaid Bronze churn 2.37% → 3.62% for four months after the July 2025 rise; higher-value and postpaid customers barely moved | avoids a temporary loss of about 1.3 points of monthly churn in the most price-sensitive group | Pricing Manager | Bronze prepaid *Churn Rate %* stays within 0.3 points of its pre-change level | Model the next change on a single county with a matched control county |
| 6 | **Run the next win-back campaign with a random hold-out group** (for example 20% of eligible customers not contacted) | q22: 1.7% vs 93.7% closure is self-selected and says nothing about incremental effect | turns win-back spend into a measurable return | CRM Analytics Lead | incremental 6-month retention (contacted vs hold-out) with a confidence interval | Agree the hold-out rule with marketing before the next campaign |

## What I would *not* recommend yet
- **Scaling win-back spend** on the basis of the acceptance gap. It is self-selected (recommendation 6 measures it properly).
- **A churn-prediction model** before these definitions and the early-warning rule are in daily use. The declining-customer rule is simple, explainable and already identifies KES 3.5M a year at run rate; a model should have to beat it.
